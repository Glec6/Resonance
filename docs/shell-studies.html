STUDY A

Orbit

The library is a ring of discs in space. The selected disc swings to the front, flattens toward you and spins up; the room takes on that game's colours. Starting a game drops the disc into the drive with a light ring.

←/→ browse✕ starttouchpad settings
STARTING

What moves

  • Discs ride a spring, so a fast flick overshoots and settles
  • A light wedge sweeps the disc surface as it spins
  • Background light fields fade to the new game's palette in 0.6 s
  • Title letters rise in one by one

On the PS4

One Vulkan pass in the launcher process: discs are textured quads with a rotating highlight, the background is three soft blobs. Box art can replace the printed label once covers download.

STUDY B

Memory unit

A big monochrome dot-matrix screen, built like the little memory cards that plugged into Dreamcast pads. Every game gets a pixel emblem, a scrolling marquee and a slow LCD ghost when the picture changes. The library list slides beside it.

What moves

  • Pixels dissolve in random order between games
  • Each dot fades at LCD speed, leaving a short ghost
  • Emblems breathe between two frames, the title scrolls
  • The list highlight springs to the selection

On the PS4

48 × 32 cells drawn as one instanced quad batch; the emblem and text are computed on the CPU, a few kilobytes per frame. The cheapest of the three, and it could also show real save icons from the memory card files.

STUDY C

Channel

Every game is a TV channel. Switching tunes through static and a vertical roll, and a broadcast lower third slides in. The picture behind it is an attract scene tinted in the game's colours, seen through scanlines. Starting a game switches the set off and on again.

What moves

  • Static burst and vertical roll on every channel change
  • Red and blue fringes split the title for a moment
  • A low-resolution attract scene scrolls under scanlines
  • Power-off collapses the picture to a line and a dot

On the PS4

The scene renders at 192 × 108 into an off-screen image and is scaled up with a scanline shader, so it costs almost nothing. The static is a noise texture shifted each frame.

STUDY D

Cover shelf

Box art stands on a glossy shelf in real 3D perspective. Neighbours turn away on each side; the selected case faces you and the room picks up its colours. Starting a game flips the case to its back, where the details sit, before the game boots. The covers here are generated stand-ins; on the console they are the real box art.

What moves

  • Cases turn and slide on one spring, with true depth
  • Each case has a soft mirror image on the shelf
  • Background light fields follow the selected cover
  • Start flips the case over to its back cover

On the PS4

Each case is two textured quads (case and reflection) under one perspective matrix; covers are decoded once and kept as small textures. Without covers it falls back to the generated cases shown here.

STUDY E

XMB

The cross bar from DolphinPS4 and Crema: categories across, items down, over a slow light wave. Items you scroll past rise above the bar, the selection sits just below it. Games, memory cards, captures and settings all live in one place.

What moves

  • Light ribbons drift and bend behind everything
  • Both axes ride springs; passed items rise above the bar
  • The selected tile grows and its details fade in
  • Opening a game brightens the wave and boots it

On the PS4

Crema already has this launcher (ported from DolphinPS4), so it is mostly reuse: the same fonts, sounds and button glyphs, with Dreamcast categories and the memory-card column added.

COVERS

Where the box art comes from

Resonance can fetch covers by itself, the way RetroArch does, from the libretro thumbnail library. Every disc carries a product number that Flycast already reads at boot, and libretro's Dreamcast database ties that number to the exact image name.

1 · Automatic

Read the product number from the disc (T13002N, MK-51117), look it up in a table of 1,897 Dreamcast discs built into the app, and download that game's box art once from thumbnails.libretro.com. Both games on your console are there.

2 · Your own

Drop any PNG or JPG into /data/Resonance/covers named after the game file or the product number. Your image always wins over a downloaded one.

3 · Fallback

No cover and no network: the shelf generates a case in the game's colours, like the ones on this page, so nothing ever shows a blank square.
